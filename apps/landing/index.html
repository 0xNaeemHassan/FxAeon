How f(x) Protocol works.

Explore f(x) Protocol ↗

One collateral, two kinds of money.

f(x) Protocol splits ETH and BTC collateral into two claims. fxUSD, its stablecoin, takes a fixed dollar share. Leveraged positions take the rest, and with it the price movement. Together they always add up to the collateral.

Say you open 1 ETH at 3× with ETH at $3,000. Fees aside, the position holds 3 ETH: 6,000 fxUSD is minted against it, and the other $3,000 is your share.

A brake before liquidation.

When a falling price lifts a position’s loan-to-value to the rebalance line, keepers repay part of its fxUSD debt with stability pool funds, and the collateral behind that debt goes to the pool. Leverage steps down and the position stays open, smaller. Liquidation remains possible: it closes what is left if rebalancing can’t keep up.

fxUSD holds its dollar in layers.

Every fxUSD is backed by collateral; it is not an algorithmic stablecoin. When its price drifts from a dollar, defenses take over in order.

  1. 1

    The stability pool steps in

    Below a dollar it buys fxUSD with USDC; above a dollar it sells fxUSD for USDC.

  2. 2

    Leverage pays to restore it

    If the pool runs low on USDC, leveraged positions pay a temporary funding cost that draws USDC back in.

  3. 3

    Minting pauses

    If fxUSD stays below its peg, new minting halts and the funding cost rises.

  4. 4

    Redemption sets a floor

    fxUSD can be redeemed for a dollar’s worth of wstETH or WBTC, minus a fee.

Where fxSAVE’s yield comes from.

Stability pool deposits earn from position opening and closing fees, staking yield on the wstETH collateral, and USDC lending. fxSAVE holds pool shares and compounds those rewards into more fxUSD and USDC. Its APY is variable.

What runs when you tap.

FxAeon is built on fifteen methods of the official f(x) Protocol SDK, and every f(x) Protocol transaction it prepares comes from one of them. Choose a screen to light up the methods it uses.

Trade uses 2 of 15 methods: increasePosition prepares its transactions; getPositions reads the state it shows.

Positions

  • getPositionsReads ETH and BTC long and short positions
  • increasePositionOpens a position or adds to one
  • reducePositionReduces or closes a position
  • adjustPositionLeverageChanges a position’s leverage

  • depositAndMintAdds collateral and borrows fxUSD
  • repayAndWithdrawRepays fxUSD and withdraws collateral

  • getFxSaveConfigReads the vault’s cooldown and fees
  • getFxSaveBalanceReads your fxSAVE shares
  • getFxSaveRedeemStatusReads a queued withdrawal’s cooldown
  • getFxSaveClaimablePreviews what a claim would pay
  • depositFxSaveDeposits fxUSD or USDC
  • withdrawFxSaveWithdraws instantly or queues a redemption
  • getRedeemTxClaims a redemption after the cooldown

Before you start.

What is f(x) Protocol?

A protocol on Ethereum that splits ETH and BTC collateral into fxUSD, a stablecoin, and leveraged positions. Its stability pool defends fxUSD’s peg and pays depositors, and fxSAVE compounds that pool’s rewards. FxAeon is built on its official SDK.

Does FxAeon hold my funds?

No. FxAeon does not receive or store private keys, and it has no account server, delegated signer, or background executor. Your wallet approves each transaction.

Which networks does it use?

Trade, Earn, and Borrow run on Ethereum. Move bridges fxUSD and fxSAVE between Ethereum and Base.

What does it cost?

Actions show estimated gas and network cost when data is available, plus any protocol fee, such as fxSAVE’s instant-redemption fee. Move shows the current LayerZero fee quote.

Can I use it outside Telegram?

Yes. The web app at fxaeon.com and the Telegram mini app offer the same actions.

What are the risks?

Leveraged positions can be rebalanced or liquidated, fxSAVE’s APY is variable, and wallets, token contracts, bridges, and networks remain external dependencies. Read the f(x) Protocol docs before you start.
